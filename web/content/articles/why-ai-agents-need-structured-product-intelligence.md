Agents **rank, filter, and explain** using machine-usable signals: normalized attributes, relationships in a product graph, and compliance/taxability facts. “Product intelligence” is the layer that makes those signals reliable at SKU scale—**catalog agents** are how enterprises produce and maintain it continuously.

Execs want “AI-ready” catalogs; teams ship hero images and PDP copy. Meanwhile **Voomi**-class programs only hold **200M+ ASIN** matching at **1M+ SKUs** because **10–15+ normalized attributes per SKU** exist—not because prose improved. **Profitero** does not hit **>95% precision/recall** on **80+ languages** from long descriptions; it hits it from **classification-ready fields. **JCPenney** marketplace facets break when supplier strings never map to **Mirakl** browse attributes—agents inherit the same fracture.

## Proof Points

- **Profitero:** weak supervision + classification pipelines across massive brand/marketplace surface—agents need the same **normalized attribute signals** that delivered **>95% precision/recall**, not prose.
- **Voomi Supply:** **10–15+ attributes per product** extraction/normalization at **1M+ SKU** scale; without structured fields, **200M+ ASIN** matching cannot stay accurate—agents inherit that constraint.
- **JCPenney:** unified catalog layer for **taxonomy alignment**—agents fail when browse-path and facet keys disagree across supplier brands.
- **Failure scenario:** **Missing voltage / pack count** → agent excludes SKU from “compare three compatible options” even with long marketing copy.
