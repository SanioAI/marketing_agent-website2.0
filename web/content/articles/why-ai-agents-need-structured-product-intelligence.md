## What agents actually read — the structured signal layer vs. prose

Let's clear up a common misconception. You might assume that enriching product descriptions will enhance AI agent performance. The truth? AI agents aren't even reading them. They're evaluating a **structured signal layer** that exists above and beyond your enhanced prose. This layer consists of normalized attributes, taxonomy edges, compliance signals, compatibility constraints, and graph relationships. These are the elements that allow AI agents to make decisions without human intervention. When your focus is solely on prose, you miss the point entirely.

Consider AI agents like those at **Profitero**, which don't classify labels based on descriptive text. Instead, they operate with precision and recall above 95% by using structured attribute vectors. This is not about adding more sentences; it's about feeding the system the right data structure. Your investment in better text doesn't translate into better AI performance because you're optimizing the wrong layer.

## What [product intelligence](/product-intelligence) is — and isn't

Product intelligence is often mistaken for robust Product Information Management (PIM) records or comprehensive descriptions. It's not. Product intelligence is a **machine-readable layer** that provides structured data attributes crucial for AI decision-making. It includes voltage, compatibility, compliance class, taxability, and fitment data — all of which are indispensable for precise agent operations.

Why does this matter? Because, without structured intelligence, **Voomi Supply's** ASIN matching would falter. Their agents sift through over 200 million ASINs not by reading product descriptions but by evaluating identity signals, such as brand canonical and key specs. Sparse structured attributes lead to lower match confidence, irrespective of how well-written your product copy is. Product intelligence isn't about more words; it's about more data.

## The five types of structured intelligence agents evaluate at decision time

So, what exactly do AI agents evaluate? Five primary types of structured intelligence are crucial:

1. **Normalized Attributes:** Standardized data points like dimensions, color, and material.
2. **Taxonomy Edges:** The structured organization that defines where a product fits within a broader category.
3. **Compliance Signals:** Regulatory data such as hazardous material classifications.
4. **Compatibility Constraints:** Information about what other products or systems this product can work with.
5. **Graph Relationships:** The interconnected data points that show product relationships and interactions.

When these elements are in place, AI agents function with unparalleled accuracy. **Profitero’s classification pipelines** exemplify this, operating with a precision/recall rate above 95% because they rely on structured attributes, not descriptions. That’s why they can reduce manual labeling hours from 140 to around 20 across more than 1,000 brands.

## Why Profitero's >95% P/R requires structured attribute inputs, not better text

The proof is in the performance. **Profitero’s** AI agents achieve over 95% precision and recall by leveraging structured attribute inputs rather than relying on richer text descriptions. Why? Because structured data is inherently more reliable and actionable for machine learning models. It enables the agents to classify and evaluate products with minimal human intervention.

Without structured intelligence, you'd be stuck in a never-ending cycle of manual adjustments and corrections. The reduction in manual labeling hours from 140 to around 20 illustrates the compound benefits of structured data. When the AI can "read" structured intelligence, it doesn’t need to guess or interpret prose. It simply knows, and that knowledge compounds over time.

## Why Voomi's ASIN matching runs on identity signals, not product copy

At **Voomi Supply**, the ASIN matching process isn't bogged down by product descriptions. Instead, it leverages identity signals like brand canonical, product type, and pack count. These structured signals are what allow Voomi's agents to efficiently sift through over 200 million ASINs with high confidence in matches. Sparse structured attributes inevitably lead to lower match confidence, regardless of how detailed your product descriptions are.

Consider a scenario where a team rewrites 50,000 product descriptions for an AI commerce initiative. The match rate and AI agent inclusion remain unchanged. The culprit? Missing voltage ratings, incorrect taxonomy paths, and non-canonical brand strings. These are gaps that no amount of prose can fill, but structured intelligence can.

## Where to start auditing your structured intelligence layer

If you're investing time and resources into enhancing product descriptions but seeing no improvement in AI agent performance, it's time to audit your structured intelligence layer. Start by examining the **taxonomy alignment** across your product categories. Misaligned taxonomy creates friction points AI agents can't easily resolve.

Next, evaluate the completeness of your **normalized attributes**. Are they comprehensive and accurate? Missing or inaccurate attributes are common culprits for AI inefficiencies. Finally, ensure that your **compliance signals and compatibility constraints** are up-to-date. These are critical for decision-making at the agent level.

Remember, every improvement in your structured intelligence layer compounds over time. Each cycle makes your catalog more accurate and your AI agents more effective. That's the power of investing in the right layer.
