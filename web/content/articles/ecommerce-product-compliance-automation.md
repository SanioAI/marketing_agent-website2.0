## Compliance as Catalog State, Not Policy Storage

In the fast-paced world of ecommerce, product compliance is often relegated to a reactive checklist, a task executed in response to policy documents gathering dust in digital archives. Yet, as marketplaces become more complex and regulatory landscapes more stringent, the traditional approach of policy storage is no longer sustainable. Compliance must evolve from static documentation to a dynamic catalog state. For ecommerce companies, this means transitioning from manual audits to a continuous classification and evidence-based approach, ensuring every SKU is channel-safe and compliant.

At Paladio.ai, we've seen firsthand how transforming compliance into a living catalog state can streamline operations and mitigate risks. Take Voomi Supply, for example, which automated hazmat compliance and multipack detection across over 1 million SKUs, reducing publish time by approximately 85%. This shift from policy storage to active catalog management is not just a strategic necessity but a competitive differentiator in today's ecommerce landscape.

## What Must Be Machine-Decidable

To achieve effective ecommerce product compliance automation, companies must focus on making certain compliance aspects machine-decidable. These aspects include hazmat classification, multipack integrity, and restricted category alignment. Each of these requires a sophisticated understanding of the product attributes and regulatory requirements, which can be distilled into algorithms for automated decision-making.

Voomi Supply's approach serves as a benchmark, demonstrating that even complex compliance issues like hazmat and multipack detection can be automated with high precision. With ~85% reduction in publish times and the ability to match over 1 million SKUs to 200 million ASINs, Voomi demonstrates how critical it is to embed compliance automation at the SKU level. This granular focus ensures that compliance is not a one-size-fits-all solution but a tailored approach to each product's unique attributes.

## Compliance Agent Architecture

The architecture of a Compliance Agent is pivotal in transforming compliance from a passive task into an active process. Unlike traditional Product Information Management (PIM) systems that rely heavily on static compliance fields, a Compliance Agent dynamically assesses and categorizes SKUs based on real-time data. This involves building a signal inventory, scoring rules and models, and bundling evidence for human review based on confidence levels.

This architecture not only accelerates compliance processes but also significantly reduces manual intervention. Profitero's implementation, for instance, achieved greater than 95% precision and recall, while reducing labeling hours from 140 to approximately 20. Such metrics underscore the efficacy of a robust compliance agent in managing high-stakes label spaces, ensuring that human oversight is reserved for only the most ambiguous cases.

## Channel-Specific Escalation

Compliance requirements are not uniform across all sales channels, necessitating a tailored approach to escalation. Selling on Amazon or Walmart involves different compliance challenges compared to a direct-to-consumer (DTC) model. Each channel has its own set of rules, making it crucial for companies to have a flexible compliance framework that can adapt to these varied demands.

At Paladio.ai, we recognize the importance of channel-specific compliance strategies. By pre-structuring channel-safe facts, we shrink the SKU surface that legal teams must review, allowing them to focus on areas where their expertise is most needed. This approach not only minimizes the risk of delisted SKUs but also optimizes marketplace performance by ensuring compliance is maintained across all channels.

## Governance: HITL Thresholds

Human-in-the-loop (HITL) thresholds are essential for maintaining a balance between automation and human oversight in compliance processes. These thresholds determine when a compliance issue should be escalated for manual review, ensuring that human expertise is applied where it adds the most value.

In practice, this involves setting confidence levels that trigger human intervention only when necessary. For instance, JCPenney's use of Mirakl and SFCC for restricted-category alignment demonstrates how automation can handle the bulk of compliance tasks, reserving human input for complex scenarios. This approach not only enhances compliance accuracy but also streamlines operations by reducing unnecessary manual checks.
