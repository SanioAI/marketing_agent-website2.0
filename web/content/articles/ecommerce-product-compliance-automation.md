## Compliance as catalog state, not policy storage

You might think compliance issues arise from teams not following policy documents. But the real problem is treating compliance as a static policy storage issue rather than a dynamic catalog state. Policies sit in PDFs, and your teams struggle to align them with real-time product data updates. This results in SKU delistings and compliance failures that hit your bottom line hard.

Compliance needs to be a living part of your catalog, not an afterthought. For instance, **Voomi Supply** automated hazmat compliance and multipack detection across **1M+ SKUs**, cutting publish time by **~85%**. This wasn't about storing policies but about transforming them into actionable, machine-decidable signals that keep your products live and compliant.

## What must be machine-decidable (hazmat, pack, restricted class)

Most teams assume human review is essential for every compliance decision. But that's a volume problem, not a tooling problem. Hazmat status, bundle integrity, and restricted categories aren't just policy details; they are SKU attributes that must be machine-decidable. 

Imagine if every SKU could automatically flag its compliance status before it hits the marketplace. The consequence? The error is caught before the listing goes live. **Profitero** demonstrated this by achieving **>95% precision/recall** in classification, reducing labeling hours from **140 to ~20** per cycle. Your team can focus on exceptions rather than the entire catalog.

## Compliance Agent architecture (vs PIM “compliance fields”)

The common failure mode is treating compliance as a series of fields in your Product Information Management (PIM) system. This approach assumes compliance is static—it's not. A **Compliance Agent** moves beyond fields, turning every SKU into a decision point with evidence bundles ready for human review.

Think of it as translating policy documents into machine-readable rules and models, which then generate an evidence bundle for each SKU. This is the same class of work that **Voomi** automated, matching **1M+ SKUs** to **200M+ ASINs** with precision. It makes compliance a dynamic process, not a static feature list.

## Channel-specific escalation (Amazon/Walmart vs DTC)

You might think a one-size-fits-all compliance strategy works across channels. But that's a recipe for SKU delisting and compliance chaos. Amazon and Walmart have different compliance requirements than your Direct-to-Consumer (DTC) channel. Each channel requires specific escalation paths.

A misclassified multipack on a regulated SKU will trigger an automated block on Amazon, necessitating a manual appeal queue. **JCPenney** navigates this complexity by aligning supplier catalogs with Mirakl and SFCC restricted categories. Your strategy should adapt, using compliance signals to guide specific channel actions, reducing friction and preserving your listings.

## Governance: HITL thresholds

The assumption that machines can handle everything is misleading. Human-in-the-loop (HITL) governance is crucial. You need thresholds where human expertise validates machine decisions. This hybrid approach prevents compliance drift and ensures that only high-confidence errors reach your legal team.

Implementing HITL thresholds provides a safety net. It means you never have to ask, “Which attribute change triggered this?” because every decision is backed by evidence. Your legal team's workload shrinks, allowing them to focus on true high-risk cases, not a deluge of false positives. This compounds over time, making your compliance process more robust with every cycle.
