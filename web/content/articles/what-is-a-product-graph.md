## Why your catalog drifts after every supplier update

You might think your catalog issues stem from poor tooling. But it's not the tools—it's the sheer **volume** of data changing hands. Every supplier update is a potential drift point. The problem isn't just adding new SKUs; it's how those SKUs relate to existing ones. The failure to maintain these relationships is why your catalog feels like a never-ending project.

**Treating catalog problems as a one-time project.** Teams believe a cleanup will solve their issues. They run a data scrub, and the catalog looks pristine—for a while. Then, new supplier files arrive. Drift creeps back in. The solution isn't more frequent cleanups; it's a system that continuously maintains data relationships.

The **Product Graph** approach moves beyond isolated SKUs. It maps out the entire network of interrelations—**compatibility, substitution, bundling**—ensuring that every update strengthens the catalog instead of derailing it.

## What your spreadsheets don’t tell you about SKUs

Spreadsheets give the illusion of control. Rows and columns might seem organized, but they miss the real essence: **relationships**. Each SKU is more than a line item; it's a node in a complex network that's invisible in flat tables.

**Ignoring the relationship layer.** Teams often overlook the hidden connections between SKUs. This neglect leads to compatibility gaps, like missing OEM↔aftermarket links. When agents lack these insights, they make flawed recommendations—like suggesting the wrong motor SKU, leading to returns.

A **product graph** captures these relationships, turning implicit connections into explicit data. This isn't just about having more data—it's about the right data. In Voomi Supply's case, the shift to a graph model allowed seamless management of **1M+ SKUs** and complex **OEM↔aftermarket** mappings, enabling safe operations across **200M+ ASINs**.

## What is a product graph, really?

You're right to be skeptical about buzzwords. But a **product graph** isn't just jargon. It represents a shift from static data to dynamic relationships. In simple terms, a product graph is a network where SKUs are nodes connected by edges that define their relationships.

**Misunderstanding graphs as glorified taxonomies.** Teams confuse product graphs with taxonomies. Yet, taxonomies only classify; graphs connect. They map out **substitutes, compatibility, and OEM** relationships, allowing agents to make **constraint-safe recommendations**—a necessity for e-commerce precision.

For instance, JCPenney's challenges with **Mirakl + SFCC** integration highlighted how wrong bundle/family behaviors arise when marketplace variant trees don't align with supplier facts. The **taxonomy alignment** acts as a foundation for attaching these intricate graph edges, ensuring consistency and accuracy.

## Why the product graph beats flat tables every time

Flat tables tell you what. Graphs tell you why and how. In commerce, knowing whether a part fits isn't enough; understanding the relationship between parts is crucial for making informed, **constraint-safe recommendations**.

**Relying on flat tables for dynamic needs.** Teams often fall back on spreadsheets for simplicity. However, this approach fails to capture the fluid nature of product relationships, leading to errors and inefficiencies as data scales.

Contrast this with Profitero's use of graphs to normalize SKU facts across **1000+ brands** and **1500+ marketplaces**. The precise mapping of attributes and identities is the baseline from which **compatibility and OEM edges** can operate with **>95% precision/recall**.

## Building a future-proof catalog with a product graph

Each update becomes an opportunity for improvement, not a risk for drift. By leveraging a product graph, you remove friction from your catalog processes. Errors are caught before listings go live, and each cycle compounds accuracy.

The **compounding effect** of a product graph means every operation refines the network, making your catalog not just a collection of SKUs, but an intelligent system that gets better with each iteration. Imagine a world where your data isn't a problem to fix, but an asset that grows in value. That's the power of a product graph.
