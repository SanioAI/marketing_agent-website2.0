## The PIM “Optional” Trap

In the realm of ecommerce, the term "optional" can often be misleading—a trap set by traditional Product Information Management (PIM) systems. While these systems allow administrators to toggle attributes as optional, this choice can inadvertently lead to significant revenue losses. In agentic commerce, where intelligent agents facilitate transactions by matching SKUs to consumer needs, these "optional" attributes become conditionally mandatory. The absence of certain data points can mean the difference between inclusion in a product consideration set and outright exclusion. For instance, **Voomi Supply** discovered that minor HVAC attributes such as electrical specifications can act as hard filters, impacting the publish-time of over **1M SKUs** and matching them against **200M+ ASINs**.

## How Agents Interpret Missing Data

The interpretation of missing data by agents is starkly different from human inference. When an agent encounters sparse attributes, it faces a binary choice: exclude the SKU or attempt risky inference. Unlike humans, who can make educated guesses, agents often use missing data as a signal for exclusion rather than uncertainty. This approach ensures precision but can inadvertently eliminate SKUs from the consideration set. **Profitero** demonstrates this with their pipelines, where vendor fields labeled as "optional" are actually integral model inputs. To achieve **>95% precision/recall** across **80+ languages**, these attributes must be treated as mandatory.

## Revenue-Sensitive Categories

Not all attributes carry the same weight, and some can significantly impact revenue. Categories like fitment, voltage, size, and pack count are particularly sensitive. Missing a critical attribute like BTU range on HVAC units, for example, can result in exclusion from automated processes. This mirrors issues seen at **Voomi Supply**, where incomplete data stalls matching for **200M+ ASINs**, and at **JCPenney**, where taxonomy alignment during multi-brand onboarding reveals "optional" attributes that are, in fact, mandatory for Mirakl's browse paths.

## Rubric: Agent-Critical vs Human-Only Optional

To navigate the pitfalls of optional attributes, it is essential to classify them by agent sensitivity. Attributes should be categorized based on their role: filter, display, or compliance. For instance, if an attribute serves as a filter in an agent’s decision-making process, it should be prioritized as agent-critical. Conversely, attributes primarily used for display purposes might remain optional. By using this rubric, businesses can better prioritize which data gaps to address, ensuring that agent-critical attributes are never left incomplete.
