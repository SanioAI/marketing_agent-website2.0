## Classify Return Drivers Linked to Fitment vs Other

In the competitive landscape of parts, accessories, and technical ecommerce, the issue of returns due to "wrong fit" stands out as a significant challenge. These returns are not merely the result of user experience (UX) shortcomings but are fundamentally rooted in catalog intelligence failures. A customer selecting an incompatible product is often a consequence of inadequate fitment data, missing attributes, or ambiguous product structures. For instance, imagine a customer purchasing a visually similar but incompatible HVAC part due to missing voltage or thread pitch data—leading inevitably to a return. Such scenarios underscore the necessity of robust catalog intelligence.

While user interface improvements have their place, they cannot substitute for comprehensive compatibility data. The reality is, returns are frequently traced back to catalog errors, such as partial coverage of SKUs or poorly defined product bundles. As observed with JCPenney, marketplace returns often stem from taxonomy misalignment rather than true fitment issues. These errors masquerade as fitment problems but are fundamentally about structuring information correctly.

## Where Catalogs Usually Lie

Catalogs often fall short in two critical areas: partial coverage and ambiguous bundles. Partial coverage occurs when not all potential compatibility scenarios are accounted for, leaving gaps that can lead to incorrect purchases. Ambiguous bundles, on the other hand, present products in a way that does not clearly delineate compatibility, leading to confusion and potential returns.

At Voomi Supply, where the scale reaches over 1M SKUs and 200M ASINs, the integration of compatibility and multipack truth is essential. Their approach, which includes automated listing processes, has reduced publish time by approximately 85%. This demonstrates that thorough pre-purchase constraint checks—such as those for voltage, thread, and pack size—are crucial for preventing incorrect selections before shipping even occurs.

## What “Good” Compatibility Data Looks Like at Checkout Time

To minimize returns, high-quality compatibility data must be available at checkout. This means more than just listing product features; it involves creating a robust product graph that encompasses all potential compatibility scenarios. Effective compatibility data answers the critical question: "Will this product work for my specific need?" It must be precise, comprehensive, and easily accessible at the point of purchase.

For example, Profitero treats fitment rows like taxonomy labels, employing weak supervision and pipelines that maintain over 95% precision and recall. This level of accuracy is crucial in reducing the likelihood of misclassification and wrong-fit picks, which are common in parts and HVAC industries. By ensuring that compatibility data is as precise as possible, ecommerce companies can significantly reduce the decision error rate at checkout.

## Agent-Mediated Commerce Raises the Bar

In the era of agent-mediated commerce, the standards for compatibility data are higher than ever. Intelligent agents, such as Paladio's Compatibility Agent and Product Graph Agent, do not accept excuses for poor catalog data. They function on the principle that missing or conflicting fitment information directly leads to wrong purchases and subsequent returns.

These agents work proactively to prevent returns by implementing advanced compatibility checks and substitution logic. They ensure that all compatibility edges are maintained, thereby shrinking the decision error rate before checkout and during post-purchase agent flows. The result is a more efficient ecommerce operation with fewer returns and higher customer satisfaction.

## Measurement Plan: Pre-Purchase Warnings, Post-Purchase Signals

Implementing a robust measurement plan is essential for reducing returns. Pre-purchase warnings can alert customers to potential compatibility issues before they complete a purchase. Post-purchase signals, on the other hand, can help identify patterns in returns that indicate catalog issues. By monitoring these signals, companies can continuously refine their catalog data to better meet customer needs.

The goal is to establish a chain causal model: by addressing missing or conflicting fitment data upfront, the likelihood of wrong purchases—and thus returns—is significantly reduced. This proactive approach not only improves the customer experience but also enhances the overall efficiency of the ecommerce operation.

## What This Means for Your Catalog

Creating a catalog that minimizes returns requires a strategic approach to compatibility data. This involves building a comprehensive product graph and maintaining high standards for data accuracy and coverage. By focusing on these areas, ecommerce companies can reduce the incidence of returns due to "wrong fit" and improve their bottom line.

For companies handling a vast number of SKUs and ASINs, like Voomi Supply and Profitero, the integration of advanced compatibility agents and automated listing processes is not just beneficial—it is essential. By adopting these technologies and strategies, businesses can ensure that their catalogs are not only accurate but also agile and responsive to the ever-changing demands of ecommerce.
