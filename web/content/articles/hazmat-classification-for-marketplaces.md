## Your SKUs Aren't the Problem—Your Hazmat Evidence Is

Most teams believe their SKUs are failing due to incorrect product data or non-compliance with marketplace rules. But it's not just the SKUs or the data. The real issue lies in the missing **evidence** that supports your SKU's compliance with **hazmat classification** across diverse marketplace landscapes. You might think it's a simple classification problem, but it's more intricate—a challenge of building and maintaining a robust evidence model that aligns with **Amazon's** workflow and **Mirakl** paths for **JCPenney**-style programs. Your internal reviews might pass, but without the right evidence, failure is inevitable.

## Why Your Catalog Drifts After Every Supplier Update

When new suppliers send products, your catalog doesn't just grow—it drifts. Teams often treat supplier data as a one-off task, assuming a single cleanup will last. The reality is different. Each supplier update introduces new data peculiarities and potential misalignments. Your catalog drifts because every new supplier file is a new opportunity for misclassification, especially when it comes to hazmat products. What disappears is the stability of your catalog; what appears is a landscape where mismatches become the norm, not the exception.

**Treating catalog problems as a one-time project.** Teams run a cleanup. The data looks right for 3 months. New suppliers send files. Drift starts again.

## The Hidden Hazmat Compliance You Didn't Know You Needed

It's not enough for a SKU to pass your internal hazmat review. It must also satisfy channel-specific rules. Your SKU might be internally compliant but fail Amazon's hazmat workflow due to mismatched evidence. Each marketplace has its own set of rules and restrictions, exemplified by **Profitero's** reach across **1500+ marketplaces**. Classification isn't static; it's dynamic, requiring a system that can adapt to each marketplace's unique policy surface.

**Misclassifying hazmat products.** Teams assume compliance across the board. Amazon has different rules. Misclassification leads to suppressed listings.

## How Multipack Interactions Compound the Problem

Multipack products add another layer of complexity. A single SKU might be compliant, but what happens when it's part of a multipack? Hazmat classification for multipacks doesn't just add complexity—it compounds it. The interaction between items requires a deeper understanding, one that goes beyond simple SKU matching. **Voomi Supply** tackled this with an automated scope covering **1M+ SKUs** and **200M+ ASINs**. The real challenge is ensuring each component of a multipack aligns with the channel’s hazmat classification—failure results in suppressed listings and lost sales.

**Ignoring multipack dynamics.** Teams overlook the compounded compliance needs. One SKU fails, the whole pack goes down. Listings get suppressed.

## The Compound Effect of Building a Unified Evidence Model

The solution lies in creating a unified evidence model that can serve as a single source of truth for your hazmat classifications. This model should bridge the gap between your internal reviews and each channel's unique requirements. **Paladio's Compliance Agent** constructs evidence bundles and **Channel Matching Agent** leverages context from **Voomi-style** programs to ensure precise alignment across marketplaces. The result? A system where every run makes your catalog more accurate, reducing errors and preventing suppressions. This is the compound effect of a system that not only adapts but improves with each cycle, turning what once felt like an insurmountable challenge into a seamless operation.
