## Why AI Catalog Compliance Still Needs Human-in-the-Loop (and Where to Put Humans)

In the fast-paced world of ecommerce and distribution, catalog compliance is a critical yet challenging task. The stakes are high, with legal and financial implications looming over any misstep. Companies are increasingly turning to AI-driven solutions to manage their vast product catalogs, but full automation without human oversight can lead to significant errors. Consider the scenario where a novel battery chemistry is incorrectly classified, resulting in improper hazmat shipments—a costly and dangerous oversight. This is why a human-in-the-loop (HITL) approach remains essential, especially in low-confidence, high-liability situations.

## The Role of Human Approval in AI-Driven Compliance

AI technologies have undeniably transformed catalog management, but they are not infallible. Automation can efficiently process the long tail of routine tasks, but the tail risk—the potential for high-impact errors—still requires human expertise. Paladio.ai champions a model where "agents propose, humans approve," focusing human attention on cases that carry a high risk of liability or require nuanced judgment.

Take, for example, Profitero's successful implementation where manual labeling was reduced from 140 to 20 hours while maintaining over 95% precision and recall. This balance between automation and expert review is crucial for businesses managing extensive product catalogs. Automated systems handle the bulk of the workload, but experts step in for critical reviews, ensuring compliance and protecting the company from potential legal issues.

## Identifying HITL Queue Triggers

Determining when human intervention is necessary requires a strategic approach. Key triggers for HITL queues should include low-confidence outputs, high-revenue products, and restricted classes such as hazardous materials. These triggers ensure that human experts are focusing their efforts where they can make the most impact.

Voomi Supply provides a compelling case study, having replaced rule-based systems and virtual assistants with automation across 1 million SKUs and 200 million ASINs. Yet, they maintain human oversight for complex decisions involving hazmat and multipack items. This strategic division of labor has resulted in an approximately 85% reduction in publish time, demonstrating the efficiency gains possible with a well-implemented HITL strategy.

## Creating an Audit Trail for Compliance

Another critical component of a robust AI catalog compliance system is a transparent audit trail. This allows companies to track decisions made by both AI and human agents, providing accountability and facilitating continuous improvement. By mapping these decisions to Compliance Agent outputs, businesses can ensure that their systems remain reliable and legally sound.

JCPenney's marketplace onboarding complexity highlights the importance of human oversight in managing brand-supplier edge cases. With the company operating across 1500+ marketplaces and 80+ languages, ensuring compliance without human input is impractical. Instead, a blend of AI efficiency and human judgment ensures that all edge cases are handled appropriately, protecting the brand from potential compliance failures.

## Avoiding the Pitfalls of Unchecked Automation

The risks of deploying unchecked AI compliance systems can be severe. Without human oversight, novel or unusual cases can lead to significant errors. For instance, a new battery chemistry misclassified as a non-hazardous item could result in regulatory breaches and safety hazards. This underscores the necessity of a HITL approach, where human expertise mitigates risk and ensures that AI outputs align with regulatory standards.

## What This Means for Your Catalog

For ecommerce and distribution companies, adopting a HITL approach to AI catalog compliance is not just a best practice—it's a strategic necessity. By focusing human resources on high-risk, low-confidence areas, businesses can achieve significant efficiency gains without sacrificing accuracy or compliance. The examples of Profitero and Voomi Supply illustrate the potential for reduced manual effort and faster publication times, while maintaining high standards of precision and recall.

As you evaluate your catalog compliance strategy, consider where human expertise can add the most value. Define clear HITL triggers, establish a robust audit trail, and ensure your AI systems are complemented by the nuanced judgment only humans can provide. This balanced approach will help safeguard your business against compliance risks while leveraging the power of AI to streamline operations.
