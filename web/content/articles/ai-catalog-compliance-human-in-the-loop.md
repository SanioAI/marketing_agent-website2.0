## Why AI Catalog Compliance Still Needs Human-in-the-Loop

Most teams think AI catalog compliance is about replacing human judgment. But the real challenge is determining where human intervention is most crucial. Legal teams often fear unchecked AI, leading to two extremes: rejecting AI outright or deploying it without safeguards. Both paths invite failure, especially in complex ecosystems like **JCPenney**'s marketplace.

### Why your catalog drifts after every supplier update

You might think your catalog drifts because of poor tooling. In reality, it's the volume of updates and the complexity they introduce. When new suppliers or products join the catalog, they often bring misalignments in data standards. This isn't just a tech problem; it's a volume problem. Each new entry can skew the taxonomy, leading to misclassification and compliance issues.

To prevent this, consider the strategy used by **Profitero**, which reduced manual labeling from **140 hours to 20** while maintaining **>95% precision/recall**. They integrate human oversight where it matters most, tackling the variability introduced with each supplier update. By doing so, you can keep your catalog aligned without drowning in manual corrections.

### Treating catalog problems as a one-time project

Teams often treat catalog compliance as a one-time cleanup. They execute a big push to align everything, and it works—for a few months. Then, new suppliers send files. The drift starts again. The problem isn't the initial alignment; it's the lack of continuous oversight.

The fix is not endless manual intervention but a strategic Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) approach. This approach focuses human judgment on high-liability areas, where AI predictions fall short. **Voomi Supply** exemplifies this by automating routine checks and reserving human review for **hazmat** and **multipack** decisions. This approach has led to a **~85% reduction in publish time**, even at a scale of **1M+ SKUs**.

### What happens when you skip the human

Skipping human oversight in AI-driven decisions can lead to serious compliance lapses. Consider a scenario with zero HITL on novel battery chemistry. The result? A wrong hazmat class is assigned, and the wrong product gets shipped. The stakes are high, and the fallout can be severe—financially and reputationally.

**JCPenney** faces similar challenges with marketplace onboarding complexity. Their solution isn't to eliminate human oversight but to position it where brand-supplier edge cases arise. This strategic placement ensures that compliance isn't just automated but also accurate.

### How to determine where humans should intervene

The question isn't whether to use human oversight but where to apply it. Look for triggers in your data that indicate low-confidence predictions or high-risk categories. Confidence thresholds, potential revenue impact, and restricted product classes should guide this decision.

In practice, this means defining HITL queue triggers, similar to **Profitero**'s model. By mapping out confidence levels and identifying high-liability areas, you create an audit trail that not only enhances compliance but also builds trust with stakeholders. This approach ensures that your compliance strategy isn't about replacing humans but optimizing their input where it matters most.

### The compound effect of strategic HITL

Every run of your catalog becomes more accurate when you implement a strategic HITL process. It's not just about maintaining compliance today but ensuring it compounds over time. With each cycle, the catalog benefits from improved accuracy and reduced errors, building a robust compliance framework that evolves with your business.

By learning from **Voomi Supply** and **Profitero**, you can harness the power of AI while maintaining the necessary human oversight. This ensures that your compliance strategy is not only efficient but also sustainable, adapting to new challenges and complexities as they arise.
