## What “Hazmat in Catalog” Means: Signals, Not Prose

In the fast-paced world of ecommerce, the margin for error is razor-thin. When it comes to hazardous materials, or hazmat, the stakes are even higher. Hazmat product classification for ecommerce catalogs is not merely a checkbox on a compliance list; it's an intricate dance of structured classifications and packaging signals. This involves understanding UN/DOT-style signals, volume specifications, aerosol and battery flags, and channel-specific restricted lists. It's not sufficient to have a "legal said yes" via email; every SKU must align with these multifaceted requirements.

The complexity is compounded by the vast array of marketplaces—over 1500+ surfaces, to be precise—where discrepancies between supplier sheets and carrier manifests can lead to costly marketplace suppressions. This is not a problem solved by guesswork or generic AI algorithms; it's a problem that demands precision, recall, and evidence-based classification. ## Multipack + Hazmat Interaction (Voomi Pattern)

One of the most intricate aspects of hazmat classification is the interaction between multipack configurations and hazmat regulations. A single-unit hazmat SKU, when listed as a multipack without detailed inner-pack data, can result in an incorrect shipping classification. This failure scenario underscores the importance of precise classification, as packing geometry can significantly alter a product's hazmat class.

Drawing from the Voomi pattern, the Compliance Agent addresses this challenge by integrating multipack considerations into its classification model. It evaluates the entire pack configuration, ensuring that each SKU is accurately classified based on its true shipping class. This holistic approach not only prevents costly errors but also streamlines the compliance process across large-scale catalog operations.
