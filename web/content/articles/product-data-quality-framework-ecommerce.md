## Why PIM Completeness Fails

In the dynamic world of ecommerce, the traditional Product Information Management (PIM) approach to data quality often misses the mark. While many organizations rely on PIM systems to ensure a high percentage of data completeness, this metric alone can be misleading. The reality is that a DQ scorecard showing 99% field completion offers little consolation when revenue leaks occur due to unseen errors such as a 3% multipack misclassification. This is where Paladio.ai presents a paradigm shift: focusing on decision correctness over field coverage.

The typical PIM completeness metric is a lagging vanity metric for AI. It might indicate that a product's information fields are filled, but it doesn't guarantee the accuracy or relevance of the data. In fact, many ecommerce executives find themselves puzzled when marketplace revenues drop despite 'green' scorecards, leading to a loss of trust in the data organization. The key lies in leveraging a product data quality framework that prioritizes the correctness of decisions made by AI catalog agents.

## KPI Dictionary with Honest Definitions

For ecommerce leaders to regain trust and achieve measurable results, it's essential to adopt a comprehensive KPI dictionary that extends beyond superficial metrics. This dictionary should include:

- **ASIN Wrong-Rate:** Measures the percentage of incorrect Amazon Standard Identification Numbers, crucial for accurate product identification.
- **Multipack Defect Rate:** Evaluates the frequency of errors in product packaging counts, which can lead to significant revenue discrepancies.
- **Hazmat False Negative Rate:** Assesses the rate of hazardous materials that are incorrectly identified as non-hazardous, posing legal and safety risks.
- **Taxonomy Misalignment Count per Brand:** Tracks the number of classification errors in product categories, which can hinder discoverability and sales.

These metrics, when accurately tracked and analyzed, reveal the true state of product data quality, enabling ecommerce companies to fine-tune their strategies effectively.

## Sampling and Evaluation Like Profitero P/R

To ensure the reliability of these KPIs, ecommerce companies can adopt a sampling and evaluation approach akin to Profitero's precision and recall methodology. Profitero achieves over 95% precision/recall, reducing labeling hours from 140 to just 20. This approach emphasizes quality through measured decisions rather than mere completion.

By employing similar sampling techniques, companies can evaluate the accuracy and relevance of their data at scale, covering over 1M SKUs and 200M ASINs across 1500+ marketplaces and 80+ languages. This rigorous evaluation process ensures that the KPIs are not only theoretically sound but also practically applicable, leading to actionable insights and improved data quality.

## Multipack and Hazmat as First-Class KPIs

For ecommerce platforms like Voomi Supply, automation on multipack, hazmat, and matching has resulted in a remarkable ~85% reduction in publish-time. Treating multipack and hazmat errors as first-class KPIs highlights the critical role they play in maintaining product data integrity.

Multipack errors can lead to incorrect pricing and inventory issues, while hazmat misclassifications pose regulatory and safety challenges. By prioritizing these KPIs, companies can preemptively address potential issues, ensuring compliance and safeguarding their brand reputation.

## JCPenney Taxonomy Defect KPI

JCPenney's approach to managing taxonomy alignment defects offers a compelling example of KPI application. By counting taxonomy alignment defects per supplier slice, JCPenney ensures that products are categorized correctly, enhancing their discoverability and aligning with consumer search patterns.

This focus on taxonomy alignment underscores the importance of precision in product categorization. As ecommerce platforms expand globally, with offerings in diverse languages and marketplaces, maintaining accurate taxonomy becomes even more critical to driving sales and customer satisfaction.
